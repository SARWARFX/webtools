Standard 5-Field & 6-Field Parsing

Standard Unix/Linux cron expressions consist of five space-separated values representing Minute (0-59), Hour (0-23), Day of Month (1-31), Month (1-12 or JAN-DEC), and Day of Week (0-6 or SUN-SAT).

  • Wildcard (*): Matches every unit in the designated field.
  • Step Values (/): Skips intervals (e.g. */15 represents every 15 minutes).
  • Range Operators (-): Encompasses sequential integers (e.g. 1-5 denotes Monday through Friday).
  • List Operators (,): Designates explicit enumerations (e.g. 1,15 triggers on the 1st and 15th).

Deterministic Timeline Simulation

Calculating upcoming executions requires handling calendar anomalies such as leap years, fluctuating month lengths (28, 30, 31 days), and daylight saving time shifts.

CyberTools iterates from the current instant through a calendar simulation matrix, determining the exact chronological matches for both your local browser timezone and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) with exact relative countdown deltas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sunday index 0 or 7?

In standard crontab, both 0 and 7 represent Sunday. CyberTools normalizes Sunday to 0 for consistent evaluation across all Unix environments.

Does this tool support 6-field cron expressions with seconds?

Yes. When six space-separated fields are provided, the first field is recognized as seconds (0-59), commonly utilized in Spring Boot and Quartz schedulers.

Are timezone calculations performed on a server?

No. All evaluations use the client browser's native JavaScript Intl.DateTimeFormat and Date objects without external API requests.

Can I copy the natural language translation?

Yes. Click the "COPY ENGLISH" button to copy the descriptive explanation directly to your clipboard for documentation or commit messages.
