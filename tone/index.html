Waveform Geometries & Harmonic Spectra

Sound synthesis relies on alternating cyclic voltage patterns translated into speaker diaphragm motion. The nature of the waveform determines its timbre:

  • Sine Wave: Contains only the pure fundamental frequency with zero harmonic overtones. Essential for speaker testing and acoustic resonance profiling.
  • Square Wave: Rich in odd-numbered harmonics (1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th...) dropping at 1/n amplitude. Characteristic of 8-bit retro sound chips and chiptune synthesizers.
  • Sawtooth Wave: Contains all integer harmonics (both even and odd) falling at 1/n. Harsh, buzzy, and piercing; ideal for subtractive synth filters and brass emulation.
  • Triangle Wave: Contains only odd harmonics like the square wave, but falling rapidly at 1/n², yielding a warm, flute-like tone with soft highs.

The Human Hearing Spectrum & Calibration

Healthy human auditory perception typically spans from 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, with peak sensitivity occurring between 2,000 Hz and 5,000 Hz according to the ISO 226 equal-loudness contours (Fletcher-Munson curves).

  • Sub-Bass (20 Hz – 60 Hz): Felt through chest cavities and floors more than heard; produced by massive subwoofers.
  • Concert Pitch A4 (440 Hz): The worldwide musical standard pitch codified by ISO 16 in 1955, derived from orchestral violin tuning.
  • Verdi Tuning (432 Hz): An alternative scientific pitch favored by classical composers to reduce vocal cord strain.
  • Ultra-Highs (15 kHz – 20 kHz): Treble frequencies that rapidly diminish with age or acoustic exposure (presbycusis).
