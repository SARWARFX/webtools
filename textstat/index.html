1. Lexical Density & Type-Token Ratio (TTR)

First codified in quantitative corpus linguistics by Jean Ure (1971) and expanded by M.A.K. Halliday (1985), lexical density quantifies the proportion of informative lexical items (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) relative to grammatical tokens (articles, prepositions, conjunctions):

// Mathematical Definition: Lexical Density (%) = (Unique Vocabulary Tokens ÷ Total Word Tokens) × 100

Spoken English dialogue typically hovers around 35–45% lexical density, whereas dense scientific reports, cryptographic specifications, and legal treatises regularly exceed 60–75%.
