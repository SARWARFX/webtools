1. Modular Arithmetic Formulations

Recorded by Roman historian Suetonius in De Vita Caesarum (Life of Julius Caesar), the Caesar cipher systematically substitutes each alphabet token by advancing down the alphabet by a fixed integer key:

Encryption: E_k(x) = (x + k) mod 26
Decryption: D_k(x) = (x - k) mod 26 = (x + 26 - k) mod 26

Where x ∈ {0, 1, ..., 25} maps to A=0, B=1, ..., Z=25, and k is the secret rotor shift offset.

2. ROT13 & ROT47 Involution Properties

Because 26 is evenly divisible by 2, setting k = 13 creates a self-inverting reciprocal cipher:

  • ROT13: Symmetrical involution where ROT13(ROT13(m)) = m. Commonly employed on Usenet forums and software spoilers to obfuscate text without requiring a separate decryptor algorithm.
  • ROT47: Extends rotation beyond Latin letters across the 94 printable ASCII characters (from exclamation mark ! at 33 to tilde ~ at 126). Rotating by 47 produces a reciprocal cipher incorporating numbers, uppercase, lowercase, and punctuation symbols.
