Mathematical fundamentals of 2×2 and 3×3 matrix transformations, determinant geometry, and computational inversion.

Determinants & Geometric Scaling Factors

The determinant of a square matrix represents the volume scaling factor of the linear transformation described by the matrix.

  • 2×2 Determinant: Computed via the cross-multiplication formula: det(A) = ad − bc for matrix [[a,b],[c,d]]. Represents the signed area of the parallelogram formed by column vectors.
  • 3×3 Rule of Sarrus / Cofactor Expansion: Computed by expanding across minors along the top row: a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg).
  • Singularity Condition: When det(A) = 0, the transformation collapses spatial dimensions into a lower-dimensional line or plane; consequently, the matrix is singular and possesses no multiplicative inverse.

Matrix Inversion & Cramer's Framework

For any non-singular square matrix A, its inverse A⁻¹ satisfies the fundamental equality A × A⁻¹ = I, where I is the identity matrix.

  • Adjugate Formulation: A⁻¹ = (1 / det(A)) × adj(A), where adj(A) is the transpose of the matrix of cofactors.
  • Computer Graphics & 3D Vectors: 3×3 and 4×4 matrices are the fundamental building blocks of 3D graphics rendering, Euler rotations, affine translation, and perspective projection pipelines.
